    "Most bands hope to strike any type of heavy emotional resonance with the listener. Many bands strive for it so overtly that it becomes self-parody. Slint managed to pull it off effortlessly time and time again. Hard to believe that a band whose recorded output amounts to under 20 songs could, in any conceivable way, be deemed one of the most influential and essential bands of a given epoch, but Slint managed it. They are to me what Nirvana are to most other people my age; the band that defined the 90s and whose influence enters into most artists I wind up being drawn to. All that in less than 20 songs."

    RECOMMENDED ALBUM: SPIDERLAND

    "Ya know how most bands who deliver their lyrics through whispers, cries and screams are cheesy or pathetic? They leave you wanting the real thing: a band that can convey the emotions that make real people actually scream in pain. Slint is the sound of that nervous paranoia. As the guitars stay toward the lower end of the sonic spectrum and keep playing variations on a theme, a sense of circling doom is always present in their music. The singer - whimpering and scared - sounds like a prisoner of the guitar's tones. And after a few listens, you'll be a prisoner too. Music for masochists." - earwax

    "Credited as the first band to chart the emotional dynamics of post-rock terrain - namely on their seminal album Spiderland - Slint shuffled down their own backroad through a lonely wilderness dotted with start-and-stop tempos and prickly guitar melodies, with only the occasional rusted signpost of punk or blues to guide the way. Their music continues to resonate long after their short-lived career, drawing its stark emotional power from unforgettable imagery and themes of memory and isolation." - eher1

    "In Spiderland they created one of the most atmospheric artistic statements of all time, the sound of a dark underbelly of Middle America that shuffles silently through the night. Combine that with the Albini-esque noise freakouts of their debut and you have a small, perfectly formed back catalogue and a whole shed of inspired young bands waiting to take their sound even further." - RadioFreeLee

    Biography

    Slint was formed in 1986 by Britt Walford, David Pajo, and Ethan Buckler. Brian McMahan joined the band shortly after its formation. Although they were teenagers at the time, the members of Slint were veterans of Louisville's indie scene, having played together in bands such as Squirrel Bait, Solution Unknown, and Maurice.

    During their brief career, Slint developed an unique sound, characterised by narrative/screaming vocals, complex guitars, and distinctive tempo changes, that became highly influential on the Post-Rock genre.

    The noisy Tweez, recorded by Steve Albini, was released to relative obscurity in 1989, while 1991's Spiderland was critically acclaimed and eventually gained the status of a classic. The band disbanded in 1990, and the members later played in bands such as Tortoise, The For Carnation, and The Breeders. Slint's short discography was finished in 1994 by the release of an untitled instrumental single, recorded in 1989.

    McMahan, Pajo, and Walford reunited as Slint in 2005 to play at the UK All Tomorrow's Parties festival. They also toured Europe and the USA.

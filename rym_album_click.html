fredde Jun 06 2026
Come Inside McCartney’s Mind
Paul McCartney’s latest album comes out at his 83rd year on Earth. It could possibly be the best album by an 80+ artist ever. Dylan’s Rough and Rowdy Ways was brilliant but recorded when Dylan was 78. The Boys of Dungeon Lane is a very solid record. No songs are weak or unnecessary. All songs fill a place. The production can be too slick and overproduced at times but it’s not the worst. I love the fact that Macca can get a few rock yells out STILL thanks to the production.

The structure of the album and the songs are impeccable and genius as ever by Macca. The songs don’t overstay their welcome and the record keeps a great pace, never becoming tiresome. The McCartney melodies are exquisite on a couple of tracks. He also has 14 songs (aka Beatles standard amount) with a thought to two halves: A-side ending halfway through with We Two and a rewinding of the tape. B-side opens with uptempo ”banger” Come Inside.

The singles Days We Left Behind (nostalgic acoustic Beatle times ballad) and Home to Us (Ringo Starr duet because why not?) are not necessarily the best tracks but they are small nuggets of happiness for Beatle fans.

Best tracks: Life Can Be Hard, Never Know, Come Inside

 Lejink Jun 03 2026
Going back to the cover photo of "Chaos and Creation in the Backyard" and the very title of "Memory Almost Full", Paul McCartney has become more and more nostalgic as he enters his old age. I'm almost twenty years younger than Macca and yet I too am finding I start more and more sentences these days with the phrase "I remember when...". Maybe like me, he has more clarity recalling the events of his childhood and young adulthood than what he did in the last couple of years!

However it had seemed to me in recent times that his musical powers were waning as witness the poorer fare he'd served up on "Egypt Station" and "McCartney III" (not to mention its ill-considered remix spin-off). With what could well prove to be his last album in his lifetime,especially remembering it took him some six years to release this, could he regain his chops again one more time?

I'm happy to say that he's pretty much pulled it off. Teamed up with producer Andrew Watt, late of the Stones, Elton John and many others, who also gets five co-writing credits, Paul has turned in an engaging album, containing melodic flair, reflective lyrics and one or three surprise musical twists.

The two singles, if they still call them that, more like advance tracks, have been cleverly chosen, as besides being definite highlights, they're highly nostalgic in their chosen subject matter. "Days We Left Behind" is a lovely, lilting reminiscence with just a hint of Johnny Cash's "Hurt" in the production and the bouncy, "Keep Calm and Carry On" spirit of "Home to Us" where he gets back to his Liverpool childhood, sharing the memory with Ringo on vocals.

Oftrn in the past, he's taken us down to his early days with the Beatles but it's interesting and refreshing to hear him go back beyond the Fabs to his loving memories of his parents in "Salesman Saint" and "Home To Us". Especially touching are his recollections of John and George in "Down South" and, one suspects, John in particular, in the pretty "We Too".

For all the wallowing in his ever present past, there's still some fire in this near 84-year-old's belly as witness the randy and raucous opener "As You Lie There" or the psychedelic-leaning "Mountain Top" with its hints of "Lucy in the Sky with a Walrus" while elsewhere he covers other bases like pop, "Ripples in a Pond", early rock and roll in "Lost Horizon" and Tin Pan Alley, via his jazz musician dad on "Life Can Be Hard".

Over 14 generous and varied tracks, it's warming and somehow life-affirming to see McCartney return to something like the levels of yesteryear. Yes, the voice betrays or perhaps more accurately reflects his advanced age, not always a bad thing of course, although one can't help but wonder if it's been bolstered a little here and there.

Still, unlike those two preceding lacklustre efforts, I think I can confidently say I'll be returning with pleasure to this album in the coming years.

 nategliebe Jun 03 2026
Paul McCartney puts together another masterpiece with this newest album "The Boys of Dungeon Lane". I was skeptical at first with the release of the single "Days We Left Behind". That track wasn't super strong in my opinion and Paul's voice struggled a bit (with the assistance of obvious auto-tune). I feared that this would the direction of the whole album. I was extremely pleased when I started my listen through of the album and found that right from track one, this assumption was incorrect. Paul kicks off this album with a bang it stays steady through the entire album.

"As You Lie There" is the type of track I didn't think I would ever hear from Paul McCartney again. It's weird, aggressive, abstract, and pushing boundaries. At his age, I have such major respect for this choice and wish more artists continued to be experimental at a late stage in their career.

My favorite tracks were "As You Lie There", "Ripples in a Pond", "Mountain Top", "We Two", "Come Inside", "Never Know", "Life Can Be Hard", "Salesman Saint", and "Momma Gets By". The other tracks aren't bad, but they just don't lean into the things I like as much. Surprisingly, the Ringo feature didn't do much for me and was underwhelming (as exciting as it was).

Throughout this album, Paul sprinkles in unique chord structures and vocal melodies, unique uses of new and familiar instruments, and gives a variety of songs that feel like a really strong and cohesive with their message. This feels like an organic Paul McCartney album. No pressured pop hits and no safe plays happen on this release. Producer Andrew Watt seems to have pushed Paul and we ended up with a great album as a result.

BrightEye14 Jun 02 2026
A new Paul McCartney album is a cause for celebration in my house. I've loved and adored Paul for almost 25 years, and I feel real gratitude and joy over the fact that this good, brilliant man is not only still with us but still working. His songbook and career matters a lot to me.

I've written before on this site about how much I love his solo career and appreciate how weird and quirky and homemade it is. People have always been quick to dismiss his genius (just recently, I read an Atlantic article that called his music 'facile') but I find his work uniquely individualistic, memorable, and--this is one of the most important bits--fun. Paul McCartney is the greatest pop songwriter of the 20th century because he loves music and he shares that with us. No one can touch him for melodic construction. The fact that his work is equal parts humane and zany adds tremendously to the charm.

The Boys of Dungeon Lane is another great one. I've spent the last few days living inside of it, not rushing through a listen to quickly throw a review up here. I think this fits in beautifully among his latter-day releases. It's my favorite since New back in 2013. When I saw that it would be leaning into nostalgia in the first previews, it did make me pause for a moment. Ringo has been trotting out references to Liverpool and Beatles lyrics for decades and it no longer feels genuine. However, this is Paul and I should have had faith. He's written nostalgic songs before and even had another album in 2005 (Chaos and Creation in the Backyard) that infused early days into its branding. And that was one of his best ever.

This is such an enjoyable listen, and I think it comes from a place of genuine and heartfelt honesty. Andrew Watt has been making the rounds producing all the classic artists the last few years. He's not my favorite producer and I do think his sound is a little bit muddy, but repeated listens have really brought out the color of the album for me.
I like the arrangements on this record very much, which were probably collaborative between Paul and Watt. I always expect little bits of surprise and wonder on a McCartney record. A sudden tempo shift. An unusual instrumental choice. A guitar solo out of nowhere. Paul played a million instruments as usual and I like the fruits of their creative partnership.

The songwriting is strong. Every song on here has great melodic ideas and the lyrics are thoughtful and sure. I like how the tracks are ordered as well. The right kind of momentum moves things along, with the softer moments interspersed with the livelier ones. It's just a sure-footed kind of record you can get from a master who has been doing this for seven decades.

I like every song here. So far my highlights are As You Lie There, Ripples in a Pond, We Two, Come Inside and Salesman Saint. As You There There is a fascinating opening track that feels like really classic McCartney to me. Different melodic ideas and sounds contrasted against one another in surprising ways. His old 'medley' style. Cool chords, an eruption of heaviness on the chorus and fascinating lyrics that recall 'No Reply' from Beatles for Sale.

Ripples in the Pond is just straight-up ear candy. The best and catchiest melody on this album and one I've gone back to for repeated play over and over. Such fun.

We Two is the song most closely and obviously about John Lennon on this record and it's excellent. A thoughtful, compelling track. (Down South is for George, Home to Us features Ringo and Days We Left Behind definitely pulls all four of them together).

Come Inside is an obvious single and a great rocker. Paul is an odd mixture of an open book and a closed mouth, always has been, so I find the lyrics on this one fun. Salesman Saint is a terrific song that pays tribute to his parents and the sacrifices necessary from ordinary people in hard times. It's got a dazzling arrangement featuring Big Band style horns (in tribute to his father and the music of their era).

I don't think there's a wasted moment here. The songs are all very good. From the coda on Mountain Top to the gentle sweetness of First Star of the Night, each one was clearly made with care. They grow on you with repeated listens and don't wear out easily. They are personal and unique to Paul McCartney. Only he could write this. His stuff has never been cookie cutter, even at its most commercial. Instead, he made the commercial soundscape fit *him.*

Paul's work is like home and it's amazing that even brand new material from him instantly carries that feeling of warmth, familiarity and safety. He's made my life better in a million little ways.

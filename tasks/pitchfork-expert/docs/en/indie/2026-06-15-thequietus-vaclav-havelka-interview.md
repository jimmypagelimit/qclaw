## A Snake Crawling Up a Broken Ladder by Václav Havelka

The idea of the artist as a networker, who exchanges impulses with other minds at long ranges, is as old as the mail art movement. Musicians aren't strangers to these mail exchanges, too; that's, after all, how the Postal Service got its name. But Prague-based musician Václav Havelka's correspondence songwriting stems from being an ultimate music fan. It dates back to his adolescence in the isolated Great Mountains on the border of the Czech lands and Poland in the 1990s, when his only communication with the outside world was through letters to his favourite music journalists in Rock & Pop, then a major Czech music magazine, but this obsessive information-hoarding, crate digging and need for discussing discoveries with others has remained in his blood ever since.

Havelka's past few records – such as the album Universal Prayer containing reimagined Indian mantras recorded under the name Golden Spine – built on correspondence with his favourite musicians and greatest inspirations, including Bill Callahan. His "penfriends" are invited to tape whatever they feel fits the skeletons of songs he sends them. His new solo album, A Snake Crawling Up a Broken Ladder, uses the same process and features overseas contributions from American guitarist Marisa Anderson, Ben Chasny of Six Organs of Admittance, Munly Jay Munly of Slim Cessna's Auto Club, Thor Harris, or cellist Vojtěch Havel, which are woven into the fabric of twelve songs, mostly guided by Havelka's lyrics tackling spirituality and how it interacts with humanism. The references to Buddhism suggest that to Havelka, the most spiritual thing is to be part of this independent music universe.

"In the end, I enjoy it the most when I make music with someone else. I have never dreamt of being the sole author," says Václav Havelka. "The song is only a skeleton. It pleases me to observe how, through these contributions, it transcends me and becomes something else." It is notable that even in interviews with the Czech press about the new album, Havelka usually spends more time talking about his collaborators than himself. "In these times, it's so important to reach out beyond national and regional boundaries, and to identify collaborators from across oceans and time zones. Václav reached out, and in the spirit of solidarity this felt like an important project to be part of", Marisa Anderson told me.

Havelka's own work spanning the past two decades is today the subject of a following at home and internationally. His long-running alt-rock band Please The Trees have played with bands like Mudhoney and received multiple Czech music awards; he appeared at KEXP with his environmentalist project fyield, and he has composed for theatre or film. But not only that: he has a rare ability to travel anywhere and have his songs travel well to all kinds of audiences. Whether it's a packed festival at SXSW in Austin, a tearoom with four people in the middle of nowhere, or to play from a deck of an old truck in a god-forgotten neighbourhood in the Northwest of Czech Republic, he will do maximum for the crowd.

A Snake Crawling Up A Broken Ladder was written at a time of recovery from spinal surgery when he was confined to bed and travel was not an option however. Song ideas were recorded on an iPhone and passed on to others, and contributions he received back were arranged by Havelka and Brandon Eggels...

Indie Rock

AKA: Independent Rock • 96,603 releases
Associated with a less mainstream-oriented and a DIY approach while often dabbling in Pop-informed melodic songwriting, eclecticism, and adopting an "authentic" or raw style.
Read more Indie rock is a subgenre of Alternative Rock associated with a less mainstream-oriented sound and a DIY ethos to recording and production. It dabbles in Pop-informed songwriting and takes influences from sound palettes ranging from Contemporary Folk to Punk. Indie rock artists often embrace an autonomous approach to achieve a more "authentic" or raw sound and write their music alone without outside help.

The term "indie" was coined in the late 1970s to refer to Punk Rock and Post-Punk bands that would release music independently in an industry dominated by major labels. One of the first successful indie releases was Buzzcocks' Spiral Scratch, which after a self-funded recording process, manufacture, and release, promptly sold out its initial run. Those first achievements inspired the creation of independent record labels such as Rough Trade Records, 4AD Records, and Mute Records.

Throughout the 1980s, many groups emerged who would greatly influence future indie rock bands, notably The Smiths and R.E.M., who popularised Jangle Pop. The latter was also part of college rock communities developed by local independent scenes. Other significant inspirations include Noise Pop and Noise Rock bands that fused noisier, rawer production with catchy songwriting, including The Jesus and Mary Chain, Hüsker Dü, Dinosaur Jr., Sonic Youth, and Pixies.

Due to the proliferation of recording equipment around the mid-late 1980s, the independent recording process and home studios became more affordable. In the early 1990s, genres such as Grunge, Shoegaze, and Britpop were influenced by the increasingly popular DIY approach, but soon after achieving commercial successes, they often diverged sonically more towards the mainstream. Slacker Rock acts like Pavement and Guided by Voices embraced the noisy, lo-fi recording and ramshackle songwriting, Slowcore groups such as Red House Painters and Low opted for slow-paced, downbeat style, and Midwest Emo acts like Sunny Day Real Estate and Cap'n Jazz fused melodic, indie sensibilities with dynamic song structures and expressive vocal styles. Around the turn of the 20th century, indie rock bands such as Modest Mouse, Arcade Fire, and Built to Spill achieved significant commercial successes and critical acclaim, and some signed to major labels.

The 2000s saw further growth of interest in indie rock. A new generation of bands dubbed Post-Punk Revival and Garage Rock Revival emerged, which adopted a back-to-basics approach, including The Strokes, The White Stripes, and Interpol, who gained mainstream appeal. Due to a shift in the industry, partially caused by the rising popularity of the Internet, bands commonly began to interact with fans directly. In 2005, Jeff Rosenstock founded Quote Unquote Records, the first donation-based record label. In 2007, Radiohead, through the release of In Rainbows, brought awareness to the pay-what-you-want model.

In the 2010s, alternative rock music retained interest through more successful indie rock acts such as Car Seat Headrest and Mitski. In addition, a wave of new "bedroom artists" also appeared, recording and releasing their music away from record label interference, usually at home.
